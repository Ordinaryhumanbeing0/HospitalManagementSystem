Chapter 1
INTRODUCTION

1.1 Introduction to Hospital Management System

Healthcare delivery in modern outpatient clinics and multi-specialty hospitals requires tight coordination among patients, physicians, front-desk receptionists, and hospital administrators. Paper registers and disconnected spreadsheets routinely introduce scheduling collisions, delayed clinical record retrieval, billing discrepancies, and weak access accountability. The Hospital Management System (HMS) is a full-stack web application developed using Next.js 15 (App Router), React 19, TypeScript, Prisma ORM, and Neon Serverless PostgreSQL to centralize and automate end-to-end hospital workflows.

The system organizes clinical and administrative operations into three synchronized worklets: Patient & User Management (Worklet 1), Appointment Scheduling & Electronic Health Records (Worklet 2), and Billing, Analytics & System Administration (Worklet 3). Role-tailored portals under /admin, /doctor, /receptionist, and /patient are protected by Edge Middleware (src/middleware.ts) and server-side RBAC guards (src/lib/auth/rbac.ts), ensuring that sensitive patient histories and financial records remain strictly scoped to authorized roles.

1.2 Aim

The aim of this project is to design, implement, and verify a secure, concurrency-safe, database-driven Hospital Management System that automates patient onboarding, doctor schedule management, conflict-free appointment booking, digital prescriptions and medical report history, itemized Indian Rupee (INR) billing, analytical reporting, and tamper-evident audit logging across a unified Next.js and PostgreSQL architecture.

1.3 Motivation

In busy Outpatient Departments (OPDs), multiple patients and receptionists often attempt to reserve the same specialist time slot concurrently. Without serialized request queuing, transactional validation, and database uniqueness guarantees, double-bookings occur and disrupt clinical schedules. Furthermore, decoupled billing and consultation workflows lead to missed charges or manual calculation errors, while unlogged record edits compromise institutional accountability. Building HMS as an integrated Engineering Design and Innovation (EDI) project provides a practical testbed to apply foundational IT subjects—including Object-Oriented Programming, Data Structures, Database Management Systems, Computer Networks, Operating Systems, Software Engineering, and Web Technology—to a real-world healthcare operations domain.

1.4 Key Objectives

  • Unified Role-Based Portal: Provide dedicated dashboards and operational pages for ADMIN, DOCTOR, RECEPTIONIST, and PATIENT roles enforced by HS256 JWT cookies and PostgreSQL-verified session checks.
  • Race-Condition-Free Appointment Scheduling: Guarantee zero double-bookings under concurrent requests by combining an in-memory linked-list FIFO queue (AppointmentRequestQueue), Prisma interactive transactions (prisma.$transaction), and a PostgreSQL partial unique index (unique_active_appointment_slot).
  • Dynamic Availability & Leave Awareness: Compute 30-minute consultation slots dynamically from DoctorSchedule shift bounds, break windows (breakStartTime–breakEndTime), active DoctorLeave dates, and non-cancelled bookings.
  • Automated Consultation-to-Billing Pipeline: Enable doctors to record diagnoses and structured medication lists in Prescription records while atomically transitioning the appointment to COMPLETED and generating an itemized Bill (consultation fee, medication charges, and 5% GST) in Indian Rupees (₹).
  • Multi-Channel Payments & Printable Invoices: Support partial and full settlements across CASH, CARD, UPI, INSURANCE, and NET_BANKING via BillingRequestQueue with browser-printable tax invoices.
  • Observability, Auditability & Recovery: Persist critical user, clinical, and financial events in AuditLog, monitor database connectivity via GET /api/health, export CSV operational reports, and provide CLI backup/restore scripts (scripts/backup-db.ts, scripts/restore-db.ts).

2.1 Literature Survey

Healthcare Management Information Systems (HMIS) have evolved from single-machine desktop applications into cloud-native, multi-tenant web platforms. Traditional hospital ERPs (such as legacy client-server deployments) rely on heavy local installations and manual synchronization across reception, pharmacy, and accounts desks. Modern open-source and commercial platforms address interoperability and digital medical records, yet vary significantly in architectural complexity, concurrency guarantees, and Indian OPD localization.

Steep deployment footprint for small clinics; lacks native Next.js full-stack type safety and built-in Indian INR OPD billing UX.
OpenEMR PHP, Apache/Nginx, MariaDB/MySQL Comprehensive outpatient scheduling, e-prescribing, and billing modules. Monolithic server-rendered PHP UI; legacy codebase lacks end-to-end static type safety and modern reactive slot-picking workflows.

Generic Academic CRUD HMS Projects Basic PHP/Express + Unindexed SQL Simple patient/doctor CRUD and login forms. Vulnerable to concurrent booking race conditions, missing RBAC verification on mutations, and no atomic consultation-to-billing pipeline.

2.2 Gap Analysis

Reviewing existing literature and open implementations highlights five concrete engineering gaps that directly motivate the architecture of the proposed Hospital Management System:

Table 2.2: Gap Analysis Between Existing Systems and Proposed HMS

Gap Dimension Limitation in Existing / Legacy Systems Proposed HMS Design Resolution
1. Slot Concurrency Check-then-insert queries outside transactions allow simultaneous requests to double-book the same doctor slot. 3-layer defense: FIFO task serialization (appointmentQueue), Prisma interactive transaction checks, and PostgreSQL partial unique index unique_active_appointment_slot.
2. Session & Role Integrity Stateless JWTs remain valid even after account deactivation or database re-seeding; client-only UI checks leak mutations. Edge middleware.ts guards paired with DB-verified requireAuth()/requireRole() session healing in every Server Action.
3. Clinical-to-Billing Silos Doctors finish consultations without notifying receptionists, causing unbilled visits or manual fee entry errors. createPrescriptionAction atomically writes the Prescription, marks the appointment COMPLETED, and auto-generates an itemized Bill.
4. Schedule & Leave Conflicts Static slot lists ignore doctor lunch breaks and ad-hoc leave dates, causing patient turn-aways. getAvailableSlotsAction dynamically filters out breakStartTime–breakEndTime and DoctorLeave intervals; addDoctorLeaveAction detects and notifies impacted patients.
5. Lightweight Full-Stack Maintainability Microservice ERPs require multiple runtimes; naive apps lack type checking and backup utilities. Single Next.js 15 + TypeScript strict codebase with Zod validation, 14-model Prisma schema, /api/health monitor, and CLI backup/restore scripts.

2.3 Problem Statement

Design and develop a Hospital Management System to automate patient registration, doctor and department management, concurrent appointment scheduling, electronic medical record and prescription tracking, itemized billing, and analytical reporting using secure web services, ACID-compliant database operations, role-based access control, application-level request queuing, and a scalable TypeScript full-stack architecture.

PROJECT REQUIREMENT SPECIFICATIONS

3.1 Introduction and Project Scope

This Software Requirement Specification (SRS) defines the functional capabilities, access-control rules, external interfaces, runtime requirements, and implementation plan for the Hospital Management System. The scope encompasses four user personas—Administrators (ADMIN), Doctors (DOCTOR), Receptionists (RECEPTIONIST), and Patients (PATIENT)—operating across 16 Next.js App Router pages, 11 Server Action modules, 1 health-monitoring REST endpoint, and a 14-table Neon PostgreSQL database.

3.2 Functional Requirements

  • FR-1 Authentication & Self-Registration: Users log in with email and password verified against bcryptjs hashes (10 salt rounds) to receive an HTTP-only HS256 JWT cookie (hms_auth_token, 7-day expiry). New patients can self-register via registerPatientAction, which atomically creates both User and Patient records.
  • FR-2 Patient, Doctor & Department Administration: Admins manage departments (createDepartmentAction, updateDepartmentAction, deleteDepartmentAction with active-doctor deletion guard), onboard doctors with specialization, qualification, and consultation fee, toggle active states, and manage patient directories.
  • FR-3 Schedule & Leave Management: Admins and Doctors (own schedule) configure weekly shifts (dayOfWeek 0–6, startTime, endTime, optional breakStartTime/breakEndTime, slotDuration) and register DoctorLeave date ranges that automatically notify patients with conflicting CONFIRMED/SCHEDULED appointments.
  • FR-4 Concurrent Appointment Lifecycle: Authenticated users query dynamic open slots via getAvailableSlotsAction and submit bookings via bookAppointmentAction. Appointments support check-in (checkInAppointmentAction), consultation start (startConsultationAction), rescheduling (rescheduleAppointmentAction), cancellation (cancelAppointmentAction), no-show marking (markNoShowAction), and doctor reassignment (reassignAppointmentAction).
  • FR-5 Prescriptions & Medical History Timeline: Doctors issue digital prescriptions (createPrescriptionAction) with diagnosis, symptoms, follow-up date, and structured medication arrays. Authorized staff upload MedicalReport metadata (9 report categories including Blood Test, MRI, X-Ray, CT Scan, ECG), and getPatientMedicalHistoryAction renders a unified chronological timeline.
  • FR-6 Itemized Billing, Payments & Analytics: Staff generate itemized bills (generateBillAction) combining consultation fees, medication charges (₹250/medication), lab scan charges (₹750/report, up to 2 matching reports), custom line items, discount percentage, and GST (default 5%). Payments are settled via recordPaymentAction across 5 payment methods with overpayment protection. Admins view operational KPIs (getAdminOperationalMetricsAction), 14-day revenue/footfall reports (getRevenueAndFootfallReportsAction), CSV exports (exportReportCsvAction), and audit logs (getAuditLogsAction).

SYSTEM IMPLEMENTATION

4.1 Proposed System Architecture

The Hospital Management System employs a three-tier full-stack architecture built inside a single Next.js 15 App Router codebase. Every request traverses well-defined security, validation, queuing, and persistence boundaries:

  1. Presentation / Client Layer (src/app/**, src/components/ui/**): Consists of 16 App Router pages sharing DashboardShell, Navbar, NotificationBell (20-second polling), DemoSwitcher (7 quick-switch demo accounts), and Modal. Printable tax invoices use browser window.print() styling in src/app/billing/page.tsx, and analytical tables export CSV via client-side Blob downloads in src/app/reports/page.tsx.
  2. Application & Business Logic Layer (src/middleware.ts, src/actions/**, src/lib/**): Edge Middleware intercepts routes matching PROTECTED_PREFIXES (/admin, /doctor, /patient, /receptionist, /dashboard) and verifies the hms_auth_token JWT using jose.jwtVerify. Server Actions validate inputs with Zod, enforce DB-verified RBAC via requireAuth() / requireRole(), serialize critical writes through singleton queues (appointmentQueue, billingQueue, notificationQueue), instantiate OOP domain models from src/lib/domain/models.ts, and record audit entries via logAudit().
  3. Data & Persistence Layer (prisma/schema.prisma, src/lib/prisma.ts, scripts/): Uses a global Prisma Client singleton connected to Neon Serverless PostgreSQL across 14 normalized models, 4 enums, and 47 PostgreSQL indexes including the partial unique index unique_active_appointment_slot.

4.2 Algorithm

Two core algorithms govern system correctness: (A) Session Healing & Role Verification (src/lib/auth/rbac.ts) and (B) Three-Layer Concurrent Appointment Booking (src/actions/appointment.ts & src/lib/queue/appointment-queue.ts).

Algorithm 1: Session Verification and Self-Healing (requireAuth())

1. Read HTTP-only cookie 'hms_auth_token' and verify HS256 signature via verifyJWT().
2. Query PostgreSQL User by session.id OR lowercased session.email (with patient & doctor relations).
3. Reject if user record does not exist or dbUser.isActive == false.
4. If dbUser.role == PATIENT and dbUser.patient == null, auto-create default Patient profile.
5. If userId, patientId, doctorId, or role drifted (e.g., after DB re-seed), re-sign and refresh JWT cookie.
6. Return hydrated SessionUser to caller; requireRole(allowedRoles) validates membership.

Figure 4.2: Authentication and Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) Flow

Algorithm 2: Concurrency-Safe Appointment Booking (bookAppointmentAction())

1. Validate input (doctorId, appointmentDate 'YYYY-MM-DD', timeSlot 'HH:mm', reason) with bookAppointmentSchema.
2. Verify caller via requireAuth(); resolve patientId (auto-creating Patient row if missing for PATIENT role).
3. Enqueue booking task into singleton appointmentQueue (FifoQueue<BookingTask>, O(1) enqueue/dequeue).
4. Inside sequential queue worker, execute prisma.$transaction(..., maxWait: 15000, timeout: 30000):
   a. Verify target Doctor exists and isActive == true.
   b. Reject if appointmentDate falls within any DoctorLeave [startDate, endDate].
   c. Verify DoctorSchedule for dayOfWeek is active and timeSlot matches generated 30-min intervals
      outside [breakStartTime, breakEndTime] and not in the past.
   d. Reject if patient already has non-CANCELLED appointment at same date & timeSlot.
   e. Reject if doctor already has non-CANCELLED appointment at same date & timeSlot.
   f. INSERT Appointment (status: CONFIRMED); PostgreSQL index unique_active_appointment_slot
      enforces uniqueness atomically at database level even across parallel server workers.
5. On commit, record logAudit('APPOINTMENT_BOOKED') and trigger notifications via notificationQueue.

Figure 4.3: Concurrent Appointment Booking Sequence with Queue & PostgreSQL Index

4.3 Methodologies

Development followed a modular, schema-first full-stack methodology:

  • Schema-First Type Safety: Prisma schema definitions generate strict TypeScript clients consumed directly by Server Actions and converted into plain JSON-safe DTOs using toPlainObject() (src/lib/utils.ts) to safely pass Decimal and Date values across React Server/Client boundaries.
  • Defense-in-Depth Security: Passwords are never stored in plaintext (10-round bcryptjs), Edge Middleware guards role route prefixes, and every Server Action independently checks session status and ownership constraints.
  • Transactional Atomicity: Multi-table mutations—such as patient registration (User + Patient), prescription completion (Prescription + Appointment + Bill), and payment settlement (Payment + Bill)—execute inside prisma.$transaction blocks.

5.2 Worklet 2: Appointment Scheduling & EHR (DSA, OS, SE)

Worklet 2 implements dynamic slot computation, concurrent booking, consultation progression, digital prescriptions, and patient medical history timelines. The custom singly linked-list FifoQueue<T> (src/lib/queue/appointment-queue.ts) maintains head, tail, and length pointers to guarantee O(1) enqueue(), dequeue(), and peek() performance. AppointmentRequestQueue wraps each booking in a promise processed sequentially via processNext() while tracking live telemetry (currentPending, totalProcessed, totalSuccessful, totalErrors, peakQueueSize) exposed to administrators via getAppointmentQueueMetricsAction.

During clinical consultations, createPrescriptionAction (aliased as completeConsultationAction in src/actions/medical-record.ts) executes a single prisma.$transaction that upserts the Prescription, marks the appointment COMPLETED, and—if no bill exists yet—automatically creates an itemized Bill combining the doctor's consultation fee, medication charges (₹250 per prescribed medicine), and 5% GST. Patients and assigned doctors inspect a unified descending clinical timeline built by getPatientMedicalHistoryAction combining prescriptions, lab reports, and completed consultations.

5.3 Worklet 3: Billing, Reports & System Administration (DBMS, Web Tech)

Worklet 3 unifies revenue collection, operational analytics, notifications, auditing, and database backup/recovery. BillingRequestQueue (src/lib/queue/billing-queue.ts) serializes generateBillAction and recordPaymentAction so concurrent payment submissions cannot corrupt paidAmount or status transitions (UNPAID → PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID). Similarly, NotificationRequestQueue serializes notification creation for NotificationBell.tsx.

Chapter 7
OTHER SPECIFICATION

7.1 Advantages

  • End-to-End Type Safety & Validation: Strict TypeScript (tsc --noEmit passing with zero errors), Zod input schemas, and toPlainObject() serialization prevent runtime shape mismatches between PostgreSQL and React components.
  • Multi-Layered Concurrency Safety: Combining appointmentQueue, Prisma transactions, and the PostgreSQL partial unique index unique_active_appointment_slot eliminates double-booking while permitting cancelled slots to be safely re-booked.
  • Self-Healing Sessions & RBAC: requireAuth() validates active status against PostgreSQL on every protected action, preventing stale JWT usage after account deactivation or re-seeding.
  • Seamless Clinical-to-Financial Automation: Completing a consultation in createPrescriptionAction automatically generates an itemized INR bill with 5% GST, reducing billing leakage.
  • Built-In Observability & Recovery: Includes AuditLog history, GET /api/health DB latency diagnostics, CSV reporting, and JSON database snapshot backup/restore scripts.

7.2 Limitations

  • Single-Process In-Memory Queues: appointmentQueue, billingQueue, and notificationQueue reside on globalThis; multi-instance serverless deployments rely on the PostgreSQL partial unique index rather than cross-node queue memory.
  • URL-Based Medical Report Metadata: MedicalReport persists external or static fileUrl links rather than binary multipart uploads to cloud object storage (such as S3 or Vercel Blob).
  • Polling-Based Notifications: NotificationBell.tsx polls getNotificationsAction every 20 seconds instead of streaming updates over WebSockets or Server-Sent Events.
  • Scope & Tooling Gaps Identified in Audit: scripts/backup-db.ts and restore-db.ts cover 13 tables but omit doctorLeave; npm run lint lacks a preconfigured ESLint config file; src/actions/report.ts initializes per-department consultations count to 0; and getBillByIdAction / recordPaymentAction scope ownership strictly for PATIENT while relying on role trust for DOCTOR.

7.3 Applications

  • Outpatient Clinics & Polyclinics: Multi-doctor scheduling with break windows, leave conflict alerts, and rapid walk-in check-in by receptionists.
  • Specialty Diagnostic & Consultation Centers: Unified patient history timelines linking prescriptions and diagnostic report categories (Blood Test, MRI, X-Ray, CT Scan, ECG).
  • Academic & Healthcare Software Training: Serves as a reference full-stack architecture demonstrating transactional concurrency control, RBAC, and coursewise IT integration.

Chapter 10
CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK

Conclusion: The Hospital Management System successfully demonstrates an integrated, full-stack healthcare operations platform uniting Patient & User Management, Concurrency-Safe Appointment Scheduling & Electronic Health Records, and Itemized Billing, Reporting & Administration. Built with Next.js 15, React 19, strict TypeScript, Zod, Prisma ORM, and Neon Serverless PostgreSQL, the system enforces defense-in-depth RBAC across 16 pages and 11 Server Action modules while guaranteeing zero double-bookings through a three-layer architecture (AppointmentRequestQueue, Prisma interactive transactions, and PostgreSQL partial unique index unique_active_appointment_slot). Static type checking (npx tsc --noEmit), schema validation (npx prisma validate), live credential checks, and 11/11 in-memory unit checks on queues, domain models, currency formatting, and JWT cryptography passed cleanly.

Future Work:

  • Cloud Object Storage for Medical Reports: Integrate pre-signed uploads (AWS S3 or Vercel Blob) with MIME-type and size validation so diagnostic PDFs and DICOM/radiology images are stored directly rather than referenced via fileUrl.
  • Distributed Queue & Real-Time Events: Replace single-process in-memory queues with Redis/Upstash locks for multi-region serverless scaling, and upgrade 20-second notification polling to Server-Sent Events (SSE) or WebSockets.
  • CI Linting & Automated Backup Hardening: Configure ESLint (eslint.config.mjs), add doctorLeave to scripts/backup-db.ts and restore-db.ts, fix department consultation count incrementation in src/actions/report.ts, and enforce doctor-level scoping in getBillByIdAction.
  • Telemedicine & HL7/FHIR Interoperability: Add video consultation links, automated SMS/email reminders, and FHIR-compliant clinical record export.
